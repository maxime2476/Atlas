Does the yield advantage of irrigated maize grow with extreme summer heat? Using French department-level yields reported separately for irrigated and rainfed grain maize (2000-2025), daily SAFRAN-ISBA weather, and heat measured on maize parcels, I compare the two modes within the same department and year. The specification was written after an exploratory analysis of 2010-2025 and before any estimate on 2000-2009; it entered the public repository together with the results, so this order rests on the author's log. In 17 departments, which exclude the South-West, each degree-day above 32 C in July-August is associated with a 1.6% wider gap in favour of irrigated maize (one-sided wild cluster bootstrap by year, p = 0.007). This meets the pre-specified rule, but 62% of the sample comes from the exploratory years. The only new evidence, 2000-2009, keeps the sign at about half the magnitude, a difference driven by 2003 (p = 0.049, power about 11%), and does not hold with heat averaged over whole departments. Without the six departments with uplands it is not significant (p = 0.146), nor with a cluster jackknife (p = 0.074). The result holds with E-OBS temperatures and satellite surface temperature, and replicates on silage maize (1.6% per degree-day, p = 0.006). With the vapour pressure deficit in the model, the association moves to the drying power of the air: it concerns hot, dry summers. A pre-specified interaction with crisis-level water restrictions has the opposite sign to the hypothesis. The result is an association, not the causal effect of irrigation. In these panels, standard errors clustered by department, or Conley with a uniform 200 km kernel, reject a true null in 16% to 56% of placebo simulations. Wheat yields over 1959-2025 and out-of-sample yield forecasts give null results.
