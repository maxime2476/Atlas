Does the yield advantage of irrigated maize grow with extreme summer heat? Using French department-level yields reported separately for irrigated and rainfed grain maize (2000-2025), daily SAFRAN-ISBA reanalysis weather, and heat measured on maize parcels, I compare the two modes within the same department and year. The specification was fixed in advance, with its hash in a private decision log. In 17 departments, each degree-day above 32 C in July-August is associated with a 1.6% wider yield gap in favour of irrigated maize (one-sided wild cluster bootstrap by year, p = 0.007; empirical size 3.7%). Rainfed yields are about 2.6 times more sensitive to heat than irrigated yields. The estimate keeps its sign on years never used before, at about half the size, but it is not significant with department-wide weather, which understates heat in mountainous departments. It is an association, not the causal effect of irrigation. Methodologically, standard errors clustered by department or Conley reject a true null in 16% to 56% of placebo simulations on these panels. Wheat yields over 1959-2025 and out-of-sample yield forecasts give null results.
