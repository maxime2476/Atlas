Does the yield advantage of irrigated maize grow with extreme summer heat? Using French department-level yields reported separately for irrigated and rainfed grain maize (2000-2025), daily SAFRAN-ISBA reanalysis weather, and heat measured on maize parcels, I compare the two modes within the same department and year. The specification was fixed in advance, after exploratory results on 2010-2025 were known; its hash is recorded in the project's decision log, published with the code. In 17 departments, each degree-day above 32 C in July-August is associated with a 1.6% wider yield gap in favour of irrigated maize (one-sided wild cluster bootstrap by year, p = 0.007; empirical size 3.7%). Rainfed yields are about 2.6 times more sensitive to heat than irrigated yields. On 2000-2009, years not used in the exploratory analysis, the estimate keeps its sign with the maize mask (1.1% against 2.5% on 2010-2025). It is not significant when heat is averaged over whole departments; I suggest, after the fact, that this measure understates the heat experienced by maize in departments with uplands. It is an association, not the causal effect of irrigation. Methodologically, standard errors clustered by department or Conley reject a true null in 16% to 56% of placebo simulations on these panels. Wheat yields over 1959-2025 and out-of-sample yield forecasts give null results.
